DeepInfer

DeepSeek has open-sourced DeepInfer, "the high-performance inference framework that powers DeepSeek's large-scale production services." It supports optimized kernels, various forms of parallelism, asynchronous scheduling, deterministic sampling and structured outputs, and is released under the Apache 2.0 license.

So now you can run DeepSeek's production inference engine yourself, provided that you have Linux, Python 3.12, CUDA 13 and either NVIDIA SM100 GPUs or Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. The published benchmarks use four SM100 GPUs for prefill and 64 for decode. It's open source in the normal sense that anyone can download it, and democratic in the sense that anyone with 68 very expensive processors can reproduce the benchmarks.

Also it is not quite a complete inference service. DeepSeek notes that the release does not include "a KV cache system," prefill/decode disaggregation or load balancing across replicas. It supports one model, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and currently offers offline inference.

This is still useful. DeepSeek is releasing the optimized kernels, model runtime, sampler runtime and much of the engine functionality it actually uses at scale. But there is a nice modern meaning of "open-sourcing our production system" that is approximately: Here is the engine, please bring the rest of the car and 64 GPUs.

Hidden help

Here is a customer-service strategy for the AI age:

WhatsApp's public privacy policy told European users that they could request erasure of their information and complain to a data protection regulator. Meanwhile, hidden text on its help pages reportedly instructed WhatsApp's support chatbot not to recommend contacting "any data protection authority, regulator or supervisory authority."

Another hidden instruction applied when a user mentioned "legal action, a lawsuit, a lawyer or a court." At that point, the chatbot was told not to reference or interpret privacy laws including GDPR, even if the user brought them up.

This is not exactly concealing the law from customers. The law remained public, WhatsApp's privacy policy named it, and the hidden instructions could not remove anyone's legal rights. It is more like optimizing the route between the customer and the law. If the customer found the privacy policy, she could learn about the regulator. If she asked the support chatbot, the chatbot was instructed not to mention it. Good customer service is about directing people to the appropriate resource, unless the appropriate resource regulates you.

There is a reasonable version of this. A company does not want its chatbot improvising legal advice, and "consult a lawyer" is often a safer answer than having a language model interpret the GDPR. But "a regulator exists" is not much of a legal interpretation, particularly when your own privacy policy says so.

The hidden notes were sent with the web pages but not displayed on users' screens. They were there for the chatbot to read. This is perhaps the natural endpoint of corporate fine print: The customer cannot see it, the computer can, and it tells the computer not to tell the customer about the other fine print.

DeepSeek Opens Its Inference Engine

github.com  ·  309 pts  ·  105 comments

DeepInfer is now Apache 2.0, exposing the machinery behind DeepSeek’s production serving stack. It brings distributed parallelism, speculative decoding, and deterministic sampling into the open—though the surrounding service architecture remains yours to build.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

DeepSeek has open-sourced DeepInfer, the high-performance inference engine used for its large-scale production services, under the Apache 2.0 license. The initial release supports the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash model on NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs, offering optimized kernels, distributed parallelism, speculative decoding, asynchronous scheduling, deterministic sampling, and structured outputs. It is designed for offline inference and includes most core engine functionality, but still requires separate KV-cache, prefill/decode disaggregation, and replica load-balancing systems for a complete production service. The release matters because it exposes substantial parts of DeepSeek’s serving infrastructure, potentially giving researchers and operators a high-performance alternative to systems such as vLLM, SGLang, and TensorRT-LLM, though its strict hardware and software requirements currently limit accessibility.

github.com  ·  288 pts  ·  91 comments

[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

Nordwand Mono is an open-source monospace font designed for coding, terminals, and Neovim, combining Univers-inspired neo-grotesque forms with proportions influenced by JetBrains Mono. Its developer created it from scratch in Python, with multiple weights, Cyrillic support, limited programming ligatures, and optional character variants exposed through OpenType features. The repository also lets users customize metrics such as x-height and cap height before generating their own font builds. It is notable as a practical, highly configurable alternative for developers who care about typography and readability in their coding environments.

OpenBSD 8.0 Extends the Secure Base

openbsd.org  ·  317 pts  ·  101 comments

OpenBSD’s 61st release adds hardware support, larger virtual machines, and refinements across networking and drivers. Beneath the release notes sits the familiar project discipline: portability, correctness, and hardening first.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

OpenBSD 8.0, released October 20, 2026, is the project’s 61st release and continues its focus on security, portability, and correctness. It expands hardware and architecture support, including amd64 kernel address space, arm64 hibernation, RISC-V vector and interrupt features, Intel Arc graphics, newer laptops, and additional SoCs, while retiring loongson support. Virtualization gains support for up to 64 guest CPUs, x2APIC, improved virtio devices, and more robust VMM handling, alongside updates to networking, HTTP services, routing, wireless drivers, and package and installer tools. The release also contains extensive hardening and vulnerability fixes across the kernel, crypto libraries, networking daemons, parsers, privilege separation, and archive handling, making it important both for new hardware users and for administrators prioritizing a secure operating system.

redact.dev  ·  465 pts  ·  137 comments

Machine-readable notes in WhatsApp help pages told a support bot not to recommend regulators or cite privacy laws in some disputes. The instructions were removed after disclosure, but the episode leaves a blunt question: whose rights does the assistant explain?

[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

The story reports that WhatsApp embedded hidden, machine-readable instructions in some help-center pages for its support chatbot, including orders not to recommend data-protection regulators or cite privacy laws after users mentioned lawyers or legal action. A separate instruction told the bot to present waiting for automatic account deletion as the only answer for some locked-out users, despite WhatsApp’s public privacy policy describing erasure rights and regulatory complaint options. The notes were invisible to users but delivered with the pages to search-engine crawlers and were removed after the researchers documented them, though it remains unknown whether the chatbot or external AI systems followed them. The incident matters because AI-specific content controls can selectively withhold legitimate consumer rights information while appearing inconsistent with a company’s published policies.

Blocking Prox1 Restores Retinal Regeneration

nature.com  ·  63 pts  ·  4 comments

In injured mice, reducing the transfer of Prox1 into Müller glia allowed those cells to produce new retinal neurons.

An antibody-fragment gene therapy showed related effects in a retinitis pigmentosa model, an intriguing preclinical result rather than a human treatment.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

Researchers found that Prox1, a protein transferred from retinal neurons into Müller glia after injury, suppresses the glia’s ability to reprogram into retinal progenitor cells—a capacity retained by fish but largely absent in mammals. In injured mice, genetically reducing Prox1 transfer enabled Müller glia to proliferate and produce new retinal neurons, while adding Prox1 blocked regeneration. An adeno-associated virus delivering an anti-Prox1 antibody fragment similarly promoted photoreceptor regeneration and delayed vision loss in a retinitis pigmentosa mouse model, with Prox1 accumulation also observed in a human RP retina. The work suggests a potential gene-therapy approach for retinal degeneration, but it remains an early preclinical finding requiring validation in human cells and more advanced disease models.

U.S. Agencies Ordered Away from DeepSeek

reuters.com  ·  281 pts  ·  241 comments

A new DHS order reportedly bars DeepSeek services from U.S. federal civilian systems. The practical boundary is still being parsed—especially for locally hosted models—but procurement, data handling, and supply-chain risk are now part of the same warning label.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

A new U.S. Department of Homeland Security order reportedly bans Chinese AI company DeepSeek’s services from federal civilian agencies and their information systems. The measure reflects concerns about using a foreign-developed AI model in government environments, where data handling, cybersecurity, supply-chain risk, and access to sensitive information are significant issues. It may affect agencies’ procurement and deployment of DeepSeek tools, while adding to broader U.S. restrictions and scrutiny of Chinese technology. The order is important because it establishes another federal boundary for generative-AI adoption and could influence how public-sector organizations assess foreign AI vendors.

Reader Reactions

The discussion centered on what the order covers and whether it can be enforced, alongside competing assessments of risks from DeepSeek and open-weight models. Some commenters viewed restrictions as prudent protection against foreign-government access or hidden model behavior; others saw them as protectionism, questioned whether open weights can be meaningfully audited, or argued that comparable risks exist across models and countries.

DeepSeek just open-sourced its inference engine

github.com  ·  309 pts  ·  105 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

DeepSeek has open-sourced DeepInfer, the high-performance inference engine used for its large-scale production services, under the Apache 2.0 license. The initial release supports the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash model on NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs, offering optimized kernels, distributed parallelism, speculative decoding, asynchronous scheduling, deterministic sampling, and structured outputs. It is designed for offline inference and includes most core engine functionality, but still requires separate KV-cache, prefill/decode disaggregation, and replica load-balancing systems for a complete production service. The release matters because it exposes substantial parts of DeepSeek’s serving infrastructure, potentially giving researchers and operators a high-performance alternative to systems such as vLLM, SGLang, and TensorRT-LLM, though its strict hardware and software requirements currently limit accessibility.

No 07  ·  Academic  ·  Regenerative Biology

Restoration of Retinal Regenerative Potential

nature.com  ·  63 pts  ·  4 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

Researchers found that Prox1, a protein transferred from retinal neurons into Müller glia after injury, suppresses the glia’s ability to reprogram into retinal progenitor cells—a capacity retained by fish but largely absent in mammals. In injured mice, genetically reducing Prox1 transfer enabled Müller glia to proliferate and produce new retinal neurons, while adding Prox1 blocked regeneration. An adeno-associated virus delivering an anti-Prox1 antibody fragment similarly promoted photoreceptor regeneration and delayed vision loss in a retinitis pigmentosa mouse model, with Prox1 accumulation also observed in a human RP retina. The work suggests a potential gene-therapy approach for retinal degeneration, but it remains an early preclinical finding requiring validation in human cells and more advanced disease models.

foxandlion.pub  ·  60 pts  ·  42 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

UK defence startups, including surveillance and counter-drone company Eyebrook Technologies, are reportedly being refused bank accounts because compliance teams classify defence work as an ESG or reputational risk. Founders and advisers attribute the problem to a combination of cautious anti-money-laundering procedures and outdated or overly broad ESG screening, which can fail to distinguish offensive weapons from defensive or dual-use technologies. Although government policy encourages greater defence investment, these banking barriers can prevent startups from accessing funding, paying suppliers, and scaling domestically. Eyebrook ultimately secured an account only after its founder contacted a bank’s CEO, highlighting a gap between senior management’s stated support for defence and decisions made by automated or lower-level compliance processes.

Reader Reactions

Commenters differed over whether the reported account denial reflects a broader banking and compliance problem or a weakly sourced, self-interested complaint. The discussion also contrasted calls to update ESG screening for defensive technologies with arguments for excluding defence and surveillance firms or leaving such decisions to governments and direct regulation. Broader threads addressed European security, political trade-offs, and whether the issue was new or relevant to Hacker News.

DeepSeek banned from U.S. federal civilian agencies under new DHS order

reuters.com  ·  281 pts  ·  241 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

A new U.S. Department of Homeland Security order reportedly bans Chinese AI company DeepSeek’s services from federal civilian agencies and their information systems. The measure reflects concerns about using a foreign-developed AI model in government environments, where data handling, cybersecurity, supply-chain risk, and access to sensitive information are significant issues. It may affect agencies’ procurement and deployment of DeepSeek tools, while adding to broader U.S. restrictions and scrutiny of Chinese technology. The order is important because it establishes another federal boundary for generative-AI adoption and could influence how public-sector organizations assess foreign AI vendors.

Reader Reactions

The discussion centered on what the order covers and whether it can be enforced, alongside competing assessments of risks from DeepSeek and open-weight models. Some commenters viewed restrictions as prudent protection against foreign-government access or hidden model behavior; others saw them as protectionism, questioned whether open weights can be meaningfully audited, or argued that comparable risks exist across models and countries.

The order’s scope is unclear or narrow — Commenters disagreed about whether “DeepSeek implementations” means services tied to DeepSeek as a company or could also cover any instance of its models, including locally hosted ones.

There is no official XScreenSaver GitHub repository

github.com  ·  22 pts  ·  15 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

XScreenSaver author Jamie Zawinski (jwz) clarifies that there is no official XScreenSaver GitHub or other public source-code repository. Official releases are distributed only as tar files through the project’s website, while contributions and change requests should be sent to him by email. Unaffiliated repositories may contain altered or outdated code and should not be treated as trustworthy. The notice is important because users and developers could otherwise mistake third-party mirrors for the authoritative project source.

Reader Reactions

Reactions split between treating the notice as a useful guard against impersonation and viewing the lack of a public source repository as an avoidable inconvenience. Commenters disagreed over whether a GitHub mirror would help or conflict with the author's choice and the project's actual setup. Smaller threads concerned the xscreensaver organization name, speculation about the author's reasons for avoiding GitHub, and nostalgia for older practices.
