Neutrality premium.

Nvidia has been in talks to acquire Hugging Face for more than $13 billion, though the companies have not reached a deal and the talks could still fall apart.

This is a nice progression. Nvidia participated in Hugging Face's 2023 funding round at a $4.5 billion valuation. Late last year, Hugging Face reportedly rejected a $500 million Nvidia investment at a $7 billion valuation because it did not want "a dominant investor that could sway decisions." Now they are discussing the cleaner governance solution in which the dominant investor owns the entire company and makes the decisions.

Hugging Face's appeal is that it sits at the center of the open-source AI ecosystem, hosting millions of models and datasets while supporting hardware from Nvidia, AMD, Intel and others. Its strength is its neutrality. Nvidia's strength is selling the chips on which everyone runs AI. The obvious strategic combination is for the neutral meeting place of the AI industry to be owned by one of the people meeting there.

Owning Hugging Face could give Nvidia a bigger foothold with developers and "potentially drive more workloads onto its chips." Sure. If you sell the shovels, it is useful to own the town square where miners exchange maps.

Runs on Chinese chips.

Meanwhile Z.ai released GLM-5.3-Flash, a 320-billion-parameter open model with 18 billion active parameters that it says approaches Claude Opus 4.8 on coding and agentic benchmarks at one-tenth the price of its predecessor.

Before release, Z.ai anonymously offered the model as "ox-alpha" on OpenCode and OpenRouter, where it became the most popular model of the week. "All of this traffic" was served on Chinese AI chips.

Z.ai says it built a dedicated inference engine for those chips and improved end-to-end serving performance by three times, reaching "per-token cost comparable to mainstream NVIDIA GPUs." Part of that work was done by a GLM-5.3-powered infrastructure agent, which helped engineers optimize the system serving GLM-5.3. The model improved the computers running the model so that the computers could run more of the model that improves the computers. Eventually the engineers can go home.

The weights are publicly available on Hugging Face. So Nvidia is discussing paying more than $13 billion for the neutral open-model platform where Chinese developers publish models that they say run economically on Chinese alternatives to Nvidia chips. If you cannot sell them the shovels, perhaps buy the map exchange.

asahilinux.org  ·  295 pts  ·  121 comments

Technical Analysis

The Asahi Linux project’s latest progress report details significant advancements in bringing full Linux support to Apple Silicon, including major milestones for M3 and M4 hardware bringup and Apple Video Decoder (AVD) functionality. Key technical achievements include implementing a new UEFI-based power management conduit to address PSCI limitations, reverse-engineering Apple’s Secure Page Table Monitor (SPTM) for M4 compatibility, and enabling direct scanout to improve graphical efficiency. These developments are critical because they overcome complex, vendor-specific hardware security and power management obstacles that previously hindered performance and stability on newer Apple SoCs. By bridging these gaps, the project continues to push the boundaries of reverse engineering, ensuring that modern Mac hardware remains a viable and performant platform for Linux users.

Tailcat enables P2P tunnels over Tailscale data planes

github.com  ·  586 pts  ·  104 comments

Tailcat provides netcat-like connectivity without requiring accounts or central control planes. It is a lightweight, ephemeral solution for establishing encrypted peer-to-peer tunnels across restrictive NAT environments.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

Tailcat is an open-source tool and Go library from Tailscale that provides netcat-like functionality over Tailscale’s data plane without requiring a Tailscale account or control plane. By leveraging WireGuard for encryption and magicsock for NAT traversal, it enables peer-to-peer tunnels that automatically fall back to DERP relays when direct connections fail. Unlike the primary Tailscale service, Tailcat is designed for lightweight, ephemeral use cases, allowing users to pipe stdin/stdout, expose local ports, or run SSH sessions without administrative privileges or complex networking configuration. This tool is significant because it democratizes Tailscale's sophisticated connectivity and relay infrastructure, offering developers a portable, decentralized way to establish secure peer-to-peer tunnels in environments where traditional VPN management is overkill.
