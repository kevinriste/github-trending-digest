Reservoir sampling.

Here is how Windows XP chose your initial user picture:

The random number generator is RtlRandomEx, seeded with the current value of GetTickCount. Windows then iterates through the pictures and uses "a one-pass random selection algorithm," replacing the current winner with each new picture at the appropriate decreasing probability.

This is reservoir sampling with a reservoir size of one. The first picture is selected automatically. The second replaces it half the time. The third replaces the winner one-third of the time. And so on, leaving every picture with an equal chance of becoming your identity.

This is all perfectly sensible. A naive approach would first count the pictures, randomly select an index and then scan the directory again to find that picture. But file-system calls are relatively expensive, and the directory might change between scans. Reservoir sampling is faster and remains correct if someone adds or removes a picture while Windows is looking.

There is also "a final safety check": Windows stops after sampling 100 pictures, to avoid pathological behavior if someone puts a million files in the Default Pictures directory.

Right. Windows XP needed to answer a simple question, "are you the soccer ball or the rubber duck?," and answered it with a statistically correct streaming algorithm that minimizes file-system access, handles concurrent directory mutation and protects against adversarially large avatar collections. Presumably if you had 101 pictures, the last one could never represent you. It was beyond the system's threat model.

Streaming basket.

A basket of Netflix, Disney+, Hulu, HBO Max, Apple TV+, Paramount+, Peacock, YouTube Premium and Spotify cost $95.91 per month in March 2021. Today it costs $154.41 per month, according to HonestlyRanked, which tracks the dated published prices.

That is a 61% increase, or $702 more per year. Apple TV+ rose from $4.99 to $14.99, Disney+ Premium from $7.99 to $18.99 and Peacock Premium Plus from $9.99 to $19.99. The whole basket now costs $1,852.92 annually.

The site is careful about methodology. March 2021 is "the first month all 9 existed," so this is not a comparison of each service's promotional launch price with its current price. YouTube TV and Prime Video are excluded because one is a live-TV bundle and the other is a membership add-on. Nothing was canceled or downgraded. It is the same nine subscriptions, mostly at the same tiers, becoming more expensive.

The original streaming proposition was roughly that you could stop paying a large monthly bill for a bundle of channels and instead pay several small monthly bills for exactly the shows you wanted. This was compelling because several small numbers feel cheaper than one large number. The natural endpoint is nine small monthly bills totaling $154.41, at which point the main innovation is that canceling your television package now requires visiting nine different account settings pages.

How Windows XP Managed Random Initial User Pictures

devblogs.microsoft.com  ·  56 pts  ·  27 comments

Early Windows engineering often demanded elegant solutions for resource-constrained environments. By implementing a single-pass reservoir sampling algorithm seeded by system ticks, developers efficiently selected user portraits without taxing the file system.

Technical Analysis

In a recent post on *The Old New Thing*, Raymond Chen explains that Windows XP assigned initial user pictures using a single-pass reservoir sampling algorithm seeded by the system's tick count. By selecting a picture as it iterates through the directory, the system avoids the inefficiency of two-pass file system scanning and remains resilient to directory changes occurring during execution. This approach, specifically a simplified case of reservoir sampling where one item is chosen from a stream, effectively balances performance with simplicity. By capping the search at 100 files, the implementation also prevents potential performance bottlenecks caused by excessively large directories, illustrating how historical Windows development prioritized both robustness and resource efficiency.

Analysis

"Relativity Park" is an interactive browser-based visualization that simulates a world where the speed of light is reduced to just 5 km/h. By scaling fundamental constants to human-perceivable speeds, the project allows users to experience complex relativistic phenomena, such as length contraction and Doppler effects, through everyday interaction. Built using WebGL 2, the simulator provides an intuitive way to observe how Einstein’s theories of relativity alter visual perception when velocity approaches light speed. This tool serves as an educational bridge, transforming abstract mathematical concepts into a tangible experience that makes complex physics more accessible to a general audience.

Analysis

The Larger Pacific Striped Octopus is an undescribed cephalopod species found in the tropical waters of the Eastern Pacific, notable for behaviors that defy traditional octopus biology. Unlike most solitary species, these octopuses form social colonies, share food and dens, and engage in intimate beak-to-beak mating rituals rather than aggressive or distance-based pairings. Furthermore, the species is capable of reproducing multiple times throughout its two-year lifespan, a significant departure from the typically terminal life cycles of other octopuses. This species is important to marine biology because it challenges long-held scientific assumptions regarding the solitary nature and reproductive strategies of octopuses, providing a rare case study of complex social interaction within the class Cephalopoda.

tailwindcss.com  ·  1044 pts  ·  402 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

Tailwind Labs has announced that it is joining Shopify to ensure the long-term stability and maintenance of the Tailwind CSS framework. By integrating into Shopify, the Tailwind team gains a large-scale, complex environment to refine their tools while continuing to support the open-source community under the existing MIT license. While the company will stop accepting new commercial customers for its auxiliary products, the core framework remains free and unchanged for its widespread user base. This acquisition is significant as it anchors one of the web's most popular styling libraries within a major platform, shifting the project's focus from independent business growth to direct application in large-scale commerce development.

What do Visa and Mastercard do? An intro to card networks

tautology.town  ·  549 pts  ·  328 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

This article provides a comprehensive overview of how card networks like Visa and Mastercard function as the central infrastructure connecting cardholders, issuers, and merchants. Rather than issuing cards or managing merchant relationships directly, these companies operate secure telecommunications networks that authorize transactions, coordinate financial settlement between banks, and enforce industry-wide rules. By setting interchange fees and establishing standardized dispute resolution processes, these networks create the trust and efficiency necessary for global digital commerce. Understanding this role is essential because it highlights how a small group of entities effectively dictates the terms, incentives, and technical standards that govern nearly all modern consumer payments.
