Jev

Okay here is the next frontier of large language models:

Take a local 0.6 billion parameter model, ask it to choose among "Legitimate," "Spam" and "Phishing," look at the logits for the tokens A, B and C, and convert them into probabilities. In the example, an email asking for your password on a non-company sign-in page is classified as phishing with 88.5% probability.

"There. That's Jev."

The post is explicitly a parody, but the parody has a point. A surprising amount of AI product innovation consists of taking something models already do, wrapping it in a new conceptual vocabulary and announcing a paradigm. Here the thing models already do is predict the next token. If the permitted next tokens represent decisions, then next-token probabilities are decision probabilities. Call it classification if you are old-fashioned; call it a "System One decision model" if you are raising money.

There are real complications. The probabilities are "not always correct," and the fancier version uses synthetic data and Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions to make them more calibrated. Also a production system needs evaluation, deployment and monitoring. But the irreducible technical insight is still: Ask the model A, B or C, and see how much it likes A, B and C.

The code is local, fast and does not send the email anywhere else. The paradigm shift is softmax.

Cheaper intelligence

OpenAI has released GPT-6 Sol and Luna, cheaper models derived from the methods behind GPT-6 Astra. The basic pitch is that Astra remains "our best model across the board," but most work does not require the best model across the board, particularly if the best model across the board is sending you a bill across the board.

Sol and Luna's API prices are 50% below the promotional pricing of their GPT-5.6 predecessors, and cached input-token reads receive a 90% discount. OpenAI also offers a Prompt Caching Dashboard and diagnostics for missed caching opportunities. The future of intelligence involves staring at a dashboard and asking why the computer thought about that paragraph twice.

The economics matter because OpenAI says its own coding-agent usage, valued at API prices, has exceeded $600 per day for the median researcher and $7,000 per day at the 90th percentile. Those are not necessarily OpenAI's actual costs, but they are useful numbers. If your AI researcher uses $7,000 per day of AI to produce AI, then artificial intelligence has achieved an important human professional milestone: generating astonishing internal chargebacks.

The benchmark pitch is mostly that Sol and Luna approach or beat more expensive models at much lower cost per task. This is the natural development of the model market. First the competition was over who had the smartest model. Then it was over who had the smartest model per dollar. Eventually the benchmark will be whether the model can complete the procurement spreadsheet explaining why you are allowed to buy the model.
