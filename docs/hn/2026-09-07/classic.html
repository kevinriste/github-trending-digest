The HN Take

Recurring knowledge

The Internet Archive would like to preserve all human knowledge forever, which turns out to involve a surprisingly recurring electricity bill:

"The mission of 'Universal Access to All Knowledge' is a commitment that goes beyond book scanners and web crawlers. It requires servers, storage, power, cooling, and the people who build and maintain our systems."

Yes. The romantic version of a universal library is that civilization writes down everything it knows and passes it along to future generations. The operational version is that someone has to keep 210 petabytes of hard drives cool.

The Internet Archive does not charge for access, sell user data or run ads, and it maintains its own systems rather than outsourcing them to corporations. This is admirable independence, though it means that its business model is approximately "store an ever-growing portion of human culture forever and periodically ask the humans for $25."

Apparently that works:

"In fact, the Internet Archive is powered by donations averaging about $25."

This month, new recurring donations are matched three to one, so a $25 monthly gift results in $75 of giving, a $50 gift becomes $150, and so on. Recurring donations are particularly appropriate here. The archive's mission is permanent, the servers' power consumption is continuous, and a one-time contribution does not permanently satisfy either one. Universal access to all knowledge is within our grasp, as long as enough credit cards remain on autopay.

Speculative tokens

Standard large language model decoding is sequential: The model generates one token, adds it to the sequence, and then generates the next one. This is reliable but slow, because even a model that intends to write something entirely predictable must discover it one token at a time.

Speculative decoding adds a junior model:

"A lightweight draft component proposes candidate future tokens, and the target model verifies those candidates before they are committed."

The junior model guesses several tokens ahead. The senior model reviews them in one pass. If the senior model accepts the first few tokens and rejects the next one, the accepted tokens survive, the senior model supplies a replacement, and everything after the rejected token is thrown away.

This preserves the target model's output behavior while potentially reducing the number of target-model decoding rounds. It is the AI equivalent of delegation: An associate drafts four sentences, the partner approves two words, changes the third and deletes the rest. This can be faster than having the partner type everything personally, depending on how often the associate is right and how expensive it was to employ the associate.

A vLLM post reports experiments with five drafting approaches on AMD Instinct MI300X and MI355X GPUs: native MTP, Gemma 4 MTP, EAGLE-3, DFlash and DSpark. Some generate candidates sequentially, some in parallel, and some combine the two. Their results varied by model family, draft checkpoint, workload, proposal length and acceptance behavior.

That last part is intuitive. Asking a faster model to guess more tokens is useful if the larger model agrees. If it does not, you have added a whole preliminary meeting before the larger model does the work itself. The future of faster artificial intelligence is smaller artificial intelligence confidently interrupting with what it thinks the larger artificial intelligence was about to say.

5. Impedance Matching

edge.org | muti | 10 points | 1 comments | discussion

First seen: September 07, 2026 | Consecutive daily streak: 1 day

Analysis

The concept of "impedance matching" describes how systems are modified to efficiently transfer energy between different media, preventing the reflections that occur when energy encounters resistance. Ranging from mechanical car transmissions to the optical properties of lenses and acoustic dampening, these matching techniques act as critical bridges that allow waves and power to flow seamlessly across boundaries. Understanding this principle is essential because it explains how physical systems, from simple megaphones to complex climate geoengineering, can be tuned to either facilitate or block the movement of energy. By viewing diverse technologies through the lens of impedance, one gains a powerful framework for analyzing and optimizing the efficiency of everything from communication devices to global heat regulation.

Comment Analysis

The discussion is sparse rather than a broad debate about impedance matching. One comment questions the publication’s apparent inactivity, while another uses geoengineering to highlight both the potential effectiveness of climate intervention and its catastrophic risks, alongside the dangers of unchecked climate change.

Austin Henley chronicles his challenge of implementing a functional Python interpreter in exactly 1024 bytes of C code. By utilizing recursive descent parsing and avoiding traditional intermediate representations, he successfully created a system capable of handling variables, loops, function definitions, and indent-based syntax. To achieve this extreme level of compression, Henley employed various "code golf" techniques, including single-letter variable names, C89 implicit typing, and creative use of operators.
