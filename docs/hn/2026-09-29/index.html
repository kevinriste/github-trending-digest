Conversational tracking

The traditional internet advertising model is that you reveal a little bit about yourself by visiting a website, and the website tells several dozen companies about it. Conversational AI improves on this model by letting you type your secrets directly into a box.

A new paper studies the web clients of nine conversational AI services and the Android clients of eight. Every service integrated at least one third-party advertising or tracking service, and the researchers identified 44 third-party organizations in total.

More interestingly:

"We show that 6/9 web and 3/8 Android clients disclose conversation URLs, titles, prompts, and screenshots to third-party services, often alongside persistent user identifiers."

Right. A conventional tracker might know that you visited a page about divorce lawyers. An AI tracker might get the conversation titled "Should I divorce my husband," plus a persistent identifier explaining exactly whose husband is under review. This is progress in attribution technology.

The paper finds that consent choices affect which tracking services activate, which is good in the limited sense that clicking "reject non-essential cookies" sometimes does something. But the basic privacy problem is peculiar to conversational AI. Chatbots generate convenient artifacts from your interactions: titles, previews, prompts, responses, screenshots, conversation identifiers and URLs. These are useful for organizing your chats. They are also useful for organizing your chats inside somebody else's advertising database.

And then there are sharing links:

"We further demonstrate that the privacy implications of these disclosures are strongly shaped by consent choices and sharing functionality, with several providers exposing entire conversations through publicly accessible permalinks lacking access controls."

A permalink lacking access controls is a somewhat elaborate way to say "public web page." You have a private conversation with an AI, click a button to share it with one person, and create a public web page that third-party trackers can read. The conversation remains private in the technical sense that nobody will find it unless they have the link, or operate one of the tracking systems that receives the link, or otherwise encounter it on the internet.

There is a popular conception of AI assistants as unusually intimate software. You ask a search engine "symptoms headache," but you tell a chatbot the complete story of your headache, medical history, anxiety, insurance coverage and difficult relationship with your manager. This richer context makes the answer more useful. It also makes the tracking more useful. The chatbot is a search engine that patiently interviews you before showing the ad.

The phone call

In 2020, a security researcher found a vulnerability that gave him access to a Brazilian federal system containing information on more than 200 million people. The records included identification documents, passports, addresses, phone numbers, parents' names and whether someone was in a witness protection program.

So he called the responsible agency.

The first person he reached was understandably skeptical and asked whether he was a civilian. A few hours later the agency called back, the researcher explained the problem, and it was fixed quickly. He says he did not exfiltrate any data.

This is arguably not a great security protocol. The system protecting the personal records of an entire country had a vulnerability, and an important layer of defense was that the person who noticed it was conscientious, found the right phone number and waited while the government decided whether to believe him.

The researcher's concern now is that AI agents can reproduce that search at enormous scale. Labs are building reinforcement-learning environments for long-horizon cybersecurity tasks, using models to create environments, generate trajectories and provide rewards. A government system that will never receive a proper AI penetration test can still be attacked by an AI trained on thousands of similar systems.

The security upside is obvious. If an agent can find a flaw thousands of times faster than a human, defenders can use it to find and fix flaws thousands of times faster. The problem is that attackers also have computers, and emerging economies may have less access to the compute and expertise needed to test everything first.

The researcher imagines an environment reproducing the system he found in 2020, with "multiple agents" entering it "thousands of times a second" until one finds the flaw. The difference, he writes, is that when the capability is deployed, "in none of the attempts does anyone pick up the phone."

For a surprisingly long time, some portion of global cybersecurity has depended on hackers having good manners. That does not obviously scale.

AI Chats Enter the Ad-Tech Pipeline

jorgegarciaherrero.com  ·  77 pts  ·  11 comments

A study of nine conversational AI services finds trackers receiving prompts, URLs, identifiers, and sometimes screenshots. Public conversation links and weak consent controls turn private-seeming exchanges into another quiet stream of behavioral data.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

The paper analyzes nine major conversational AI services and finds that all integrated third-party advertising, analytics, or tracking services across their web or Android clients. In six web services and three Android apps, trackers received conversation-related data such as URLs, titles, prompts, identifiers, or screenshots, sometimes alongside cookies, hashed emails, advertising IDs, or account information. Some providers also used publicly accessible conversation links, allowing anyone with the URL—and potentially embedded trackers—to view entire chats, while consent rejection and paid subscriptions often provided limited protection. The findings matter because AI conversations can contain unusually sensitive personal and professional information, extending familiar ad-tech data flows into interactions users may reasonably expect to be private and raising potential GDPR and ePrivacy concerns.

Link Any C++ Library Into Godot

blog.conan.io  ·  47 pts  ·  16 comments

Use GDExtension. Bind through godot-cpp. Let Conan and CMake handle versions, targets, and dependencies. The example drives 100,000 particles with flecs, while platform packaging remains your problem.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

The article explains how to use arbitrary C and C++ libraries in Godot games by writing GDExtensions with the official godot-cpp bindings, rather than modifying or rebuilding the engine. It presents Conan’s godot-cpp package and CMake integration as a way to manage Godot-version compatibility, build targets, third-party dependencies, and platform-specific binaries. This matters because it turns native-library integration into a more conventional, reproducible C++ build process, though developers still need to account for per-platform distribution and runtime library compatibility.

A Sunflower Turns Sound Into Light

jagi.studio  ·  155 pts  ·  17 comments

Phyllotaxis becomes hardware: printed cell walls, diffusing paper, an 89-LED matrix, and a microphone-driven signal chain. The build moves from STM32 experiments to modular PCBs and an ESP32 installation at the Recurse Center.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

The article documents the construction of an audio-reactive LED display based on phyllotaxis, the sunflower-like pattern generated by rotating points according to the golden ratio and then Voronoi-tessellating them. The creator converted the digital geometry into 3D-printed cell walls, diffusing paper, and an 89-LED RGB matrix controlled initially by an STM32, with a microphone, Fourier analysis, and multiband audio processing driving the animations. A later version replaced much of the custom structure with modular PCBs, moved to an ESP32, and added Wi-Fi sketch uploads through a local web interface before being installed at the Recurse Center. The project is notable as an example of integrating procedural graphics, fabrication, embedded DSP, PCB design, and interactive software, while also exposing the manufacturing challenges that would need to be solved before producing more units.

computerworld.com  ·  62 pts  ·  20 comments

Technical Analysis

The story profiles a Stratus fault-tolerant server that had been running continuously at Great Lakes Works since 1993, with roughly 80% of its original hardware still in service. Its redundant components, stable proprietary VOS operating system, and replacement parts helped it avoid unplanned downtime for 24 years, despite lacking modern software and maintenance support. The Voxel Engine Learns to Hide

enikofox.com  ·  23 pts  ·  2 comments

A software depth buffer and hierarchical chunk maps let a block game discard geometry the player cannot see. Careful transforms and screen-space tests cut culling time from roughly 200 milliseconds to about four.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

The article describes a software-based occlusion culling system for a voxel “Block Game,” designed to improve performance on integrated or older GPUs without relying on modern GPU depth-buffer features. It renders a low-resolution CPU depth buffer, represents opaque block geometry through hierarchical subchunk “mipmaps,” and uses world-space frustum culling plus optimized screen-space tests to discard chunks hidden behind nearer geometry. Iterative optimizations—including cheaper transforms, excluding chunks without visible faces, distance-based processing, and multiple subchunk levels—reduced culling time from roughly 150–200 ms to about 4 ms at a 12-chunk render distance. The method typically culls at least half of post-frustum chunks and up to 95% in caves or interiors, though it is less effective in open areas or around thin, distant structures.

Wine Grapes Lose Their Buyers

kqed.org  ·  235 pts  ·  583 comments

California growers are harvesting into a market that no longer wants the same volume of wine. Falling consumption, competition from other drinks, tariffs, and high costs leave farms choosing between losses, idle vines, and conversion.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

California wine-grape growers are facing a sustained demand slump, with U.S. wine sales falling more than 20% in five years and roughly a quarter of the state’s vineyards removed or left inactive. About half of this year’s crop entered harvest without buyers, forcing farmers to sell at a loss, leave grapes unpicked, or replace vines with more profitable crops. The downturn reflects changing consumer habits, competition from beer, spirits, cocktails and cannabis, weaker global consumption, higher U.S. production costs, and tariffs that constrain exports. The crisis threatens multigenerational farms and reduces income for farmworkers and local businesses while the industry works to rebalance oversupply with changing tastes.

Analysis

The article describes a software-based occlusion culling system for a voxel “Block Game,” designed to improve performance on integrated or older GPUs without relying on modern GPU depth-buffer features. Analysis

California wine-grape growers are facing a sustained demand slump, with U.S. wine sales falling more than 20% in five years and roughly a quarter of the state’s vineyards removed or left inactive. About half of this year’s crop entered harvest without buyers, forcing farmers to sell at a loss, leave grapes unpicked, or replace vines with more profitable crops. stateofutopia.com  ·  248 pts  ·  86 comments  ·  discussion

Analysis

MicroLLM Lab is an in-browser benchmarking tool that lets users run and compare seven very small language models, including a 135M-parameter model, on standardized objective tests. It reports per-test accuracy, sustained token-generation speed, and suite runtime using the user’s own hardware, with estimates adjusted after local runs. The site also supports custom JavaScript benchmarks, downloadable verifiable performance certificates, and social sharing of results. Its significance is that it makes lightweight model capabilities and hardware trade-offs measurable locally, while emphasizing that low-capacity models may fail and that those failures are part of the evaluation.
