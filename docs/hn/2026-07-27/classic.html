In the 1970s, Douglas McIlroy optimized the Unix `spell` command to fit a 30,000-word dictionary into just 64 kB of RAM, a challenge that required moving beyond standard compression techniques. After initial experiments with a Bloom filter, McIlroy developed a specialized approach by sorting hash codes and applying a delta-based compression scheme to the differences between them. By recognizing that these hash differences followed a geometric distribution, he implemented Golomb coding to achieve a compression density within 0.03 bits of the theoretical limit. This feat remains a notable example of engineering under extreme hardware constraints, demonstrating how mathematical modeling and first-principles design can yield highly efficient, long-lasting solutions.
