The HN Take

Catch

SpaceX launched Starship from Texas, put it into orbit, deployed 26 Starlink satellites, kept it there for more than 20 hours, brought it back through the atmosphere and caught it with the launch tower:

After breaking through the clouds, three Raptor engines ignited for a landing flip and slow descent into Mechzilla's waiting arms, which closed to catch Starship for the very first time.

Sure. The basic dream of reusable rockets is that they should work less like missiles and more like airplanes. You take off, deliver your passengers or satellites, come back to the same place and get ready to go again. Airplanes normally accomplish the last step by landing on the ground. SpaceX's approach is to omit the landing gear and have the airport grab the airplane out of the sky with giant mechanical arms. Same basic idea, visually different.

The ship left Starbase and returned to the same launch site 22.5 hours later. In between, it went to orbit, delivered 26 satellites and circled Earth for most of a day. This is an unusually elaborate way to return to your parking spot, though it did get some work done along the way.

The booster had a less tidy trip. It relit 32 of 33 engines for its boostback burn, ended the burn early because of low fuel mass, automatically aborted its return and diverted to an offshore landing location. It then hovered over the Gulf, shut down its engines and triggered its flight termination system.

The timeline nevertheless calls that event "Super Heavy landing burn shutdown and catch." This is either a leftover description or a fairly expansive definition of "catch." The ocean caught it.

Lovable security

Here is one version of AI democratizing software development:

Researchers examined 159,041 projects built with Lovable, the platform that turns plain-language descriptions into web applications. Of those, 34,545 used Supabase. The researchers looked more closely at 24,664 sites that sent database records when they loaded, then made additional read-only requests using the same API keys.

They found records containing email addresses in 10,560 tables across 7,403 sites. They also found more than 200,000 private chat messages from one forum, identity-document details including passport numbers from a service provider, internal email summaries from a property manager, and tens of thousands of contracts, invoices and personal photos.

The researchers are careful to say that their sample is not representative, that email addresses can be public or fake, and that the 30% figure "should therefore not be read as a confirmed data leak rate." Fine. Some of the email addresses might be harmless. The passport numbers are more difficult to explain as website decoration.

Lovable markets itself as requiring no technical knowledge, but its terms put responsibility for applications and data handling on customers. This is a reasonable legal arrangement and a funny product arrangement. You describe the app in ordinary language, the AI builds it, and then you hire a professional to determine whether it has accidentally published everyone's passports.

"An easy-to-use development platform does not remove the need for an expert review of access permissions before an application handles confidential data," the researchers conclude. Right. AI has democratized building the application. Securing the application remains an enterprise feature.

Analysis

SpaceX’s Starship Flight 15 launched from Starbase, deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites in orbit, and returned roughly 22.5 hours later for the first successful catch of the spacecraft by the launch tower’s mechanical arms. The Super Heavy booster completed ascent and most of its boostback but aborted its planned trajectory after an early shutdown caused by low fuel, then landed offshore before its flight-termination system was activated. Starship performed its orbital insertion, satellite deployment, deorbit burn, atmospheric reentry, and powered landing largely as planned. The flight is important because recovering both stages—especially catching the spacecraft at the launch site—represents a major step toward SpaceX’s goal of rapidly reusable vehicles and could reduce launch costs while supporting the company’s Starlink and future lunar ambitions.

3. Lovable apps expose passport details and more than 200K private messages

crissyfield.de | armcat | 851 points | 324 comments | discussion

First seen: November 15, 2026 | Consecutive daily streak: 1 day

Analysis

Security researchers analyzed 159,041 applications built with the AI development platform Lovable and found that many using Supabase databases exposed records without requiring authentication. Among 24,664 sites that sent database data on load, further read-only requests revealed email-containing records in 10,560 tables across 7,403 sites, though the researchers caution this is not a representative leak rate. Exposed information included passport details, customer and tenant data, payment and ticket records, contracts, private messages, and hundreds of thousands of emails. The findings highlight that AI-assisted app creation does not automatically configure secure access controls, leaving nontechnical users responsible for expert security reviews before handling confidential data.

Analysis

tsoracle is an embeddable Rust service that provides strictly increasing timestamps and durable, gapless integer sequences for distributed applications. It addresses the limits of host clocks and database sequences in use cases such as MVCC ordering, cross-shard change streams, audit logs, invoices, and ledger numbering. Analysis

The article explains how to configure NetBSD disk encryption to protect data if a device is lost or stolen, while distinguishing this goal from the much harder problem of detecting and resisting hardware tampering. It argues that encrypting only `/home` may leave sensitive logs, crash dumps, configurations, packages, and system data exposed, and presents a middle-ground design with a small unencrypted root filesystem and encrypted `/usr`, `/var`, and `/home` volumes using NetBSD’s CGD framework.
