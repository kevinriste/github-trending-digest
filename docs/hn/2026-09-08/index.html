Shared resilience

A content delivery network is supposed to make your website more resilient. CipherCue looked at 44,143 CDN-using companies across eight European countries and found that 39,547 of them, or 89.6%, use Cloudflare.

This is not a representative sample of every European company. It skews toward small and midsized businesses, where Cloudflare's free tier is particularly strong, and it excludes companies with no detectable CDN. Still: If you are a European company in this sample that has decided to pay someone to keep your website online, there is nearly a nine-in-ten chance that you picked the same someone as everyone else.

The point of outsourcing resilience is that Cloudflare is presumably better at keeping websites online than each customer would be individually. The problem with outsourcing resilience is that everyone outsourced it to Cloudflare. Every company independently reduced its own operational risk and collectively created one enormous operational risk. Diversification, but backwards.

CipherCue points to three global Cloudflare incidents in the last 15 months. One involved a feature file that doubled in size, one involved a configuration change made while responding to a vulnerability, and one involved cleanup automation withdrawing internet routes. Cloudflare emphasized that the incidents were "not caused, directly or indirectly, by a cyber attack or malicious activity of any kind."

Sure. If one hacker took down most of Europe's CDN-using small businesses, that would be an alarming demonstration of the hacker's power. If one cleanup task does it, that is normal maintenance.

"This is not about Cloudflare being sloppy," CipherCue writes. Fastly and Akamai have bugs too. They just have far fewer customers, so their bugs are less macroeconomically significant. Cloudflare's product is resilience for individual websites and synchronized downtime for the European web. Everyone bought an independent backup and got the same backup.

Nearly 90 Percent of European CDNs Use Cloudflare

ciphercue.com  ·  125 pts  ·  128 comments

A high degree of concentration among web infrastructure providers creates systemic risks for the digital market. Even minor internal configuration errors can trigger widespread outages for businesses that rely on a single dominant platform.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

A recent analysis by CipherCue reveals that nearly 90% of European companies using a content delivery network (CDN) rely on Cloudflare, highlighting a significant market concentration among web infrastructure providers. While CDNs are intended to improve resilience and performance, this heavy reliance creates a single point of failure where internal configuration errors at the provider can trigger widespread, simultaneous outages for a vast number of unrelated businesses. The study clarifies that while CDNs act as the public-facing "front door" for these websites, they are distinct from the origin servers where data residency—a critical factor for European GDPR compliance—is actually managed. Ultimately, this high level of dependency illustrates the systemic risk inherent in relying on a dominant infrastructure provider, as the provider's technical mistakes increasingly become shared vulnerabilities for the entire digital market.

Factoring 1990s RSA Keys With Modern Hardware

mcpherrin.ca  ·  357 pts  ·  59 comments

Modern computing power turns early web security standards into a trivial exercise. Breaking these legacy keys demonstrates the massive evolution in cryptographic vulnerability over the last three decades.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

Security researcher Matt McPherrin successfully factored 512-bit RSA keys from obsolete root certificate authorities that shipped with early 1990s browsers like Netscape 4.51. By utilizing the CADO-NFS software on a modern desktop, he demonstrated that these historically weak cryptographic standards are easily breakable today. This project highlights the massive evolution of computational power and cryptographic security since the inception of the Web PKI, when export restrictions and a lack of standardized requirements led to the use of insecure, low-entropy keys. While these specific certificates are no longer trusted by modern systems, the experiment serves as a technical case study on the inherent vulnerabilities of legacy encryption standards.

Diverse Soil Contact Improves Human Immune Regulation

bbc.com  ·  27 pts  ·  21 comments

Frequent contact with natural environments directly shifts the composition of the human microbiome.

Increasing biodiversity in our daily routines offers a measurable strategy to lower the risk of inflammatory conditions.

Read →Share →
[ Analysis + ]

Technical Analysis

Recent research suggests that regular skin contact with diverse natural environments, such as soil, moss, and forest floors, significantly boosts the microbial diversity of the human microbiome. By introducing a rich variety of beneficial microbes, this exposure helps crowd out potential pathogens and promotes better immune system regulation, potentially lowering the risk of inflammatory and autoimmune conditions. Studies involving both children in outdoor daycare settings and adults engaged in indoor gardening demonstrate that these biological shifts can occur within weeks of consistent nature contact. These findings support the biodiversity hypothesis, highlighting a low-risk, accessible strategy to improve public health by integrating more natural, microbe-rich elements into urban environments and daily routines.
