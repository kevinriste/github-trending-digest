The HN Take

Pentium II.

A new Mac Mini can run an old PC quite fast, provided you are willing to be flexible about what "old PC" means. A reviewer used 86Box to emulate a Pentium II running Windows 98 with a Voodoo 3 graphics card. The M6 Mac Mini sustained an emulated clock of 600MHz through Cinebench and a 3DMark demo without the audio dropping out. The M4 managed 500MHz.

This is a demanding test because 86Box tries to reproduce the machine's hardware timing, and most of the work falls on one host thread. The reviewer extended the emulator's Pentium II clock settings, then ran Winamp in the background so that falling behind would be audible. A render that finished but briefly made the music hitch did not count. Fair enough: An authentic Windows 98 experience should have its glitches for the right reasons.

There is a wrinkle. The emulated 600MHz Pentium II scored about the same in Cinebench 2000 as a user-reported physical 800MHz Pentium III. Even at lower clocks, the emulator substantially outscored the period Pentium II results the reviewer found. Those are comparisons across different machines, not controlled measurements, and the reviewer does not claim the virtual computer performs like the real thing. Still, the achievement is slightly hard to describe: The Mac is fast enough to emulate a Pentium II at 600MHz, except the resulting Pentium II may be rather better at Cinebench than a Pentium II.

F-Droid 2.0.

F-Droid has released its biggest Android app update in 10 years. The open-source app store has better search, more detailed categories and a redesigned Discover screen. It also says that, thanks largely to regulatory pressure, changes to Android now let alternative app stores offer a smoother installation experience.

This is the fairly concrete version of app-store competition. It is one thing to say users are free to install a rival store; it is another to make installing an app from that store feel reasonably like installing one from the built-in store. F-Droid says its new installer lets users approve an installation when they choose it, rather than waiting until after the download. Not glamorous, but neither is being interrupted twice to install a password manager.

The redesign also makes a less cheerful usability choice. F-Droid used to offer a panic-triggered feature that could remove and wipe apps. It has not returned in version 2.0: The project says the specialized work needed to maintain it, for a small user base, was holding up other improvements. Users who rely on it may want to postpone updating. F-Droid is trying to make software work better for thousands of apps and many kinds of users; one consequence is that an emergency feature for a few users is, for now, easier to find in the old version.
