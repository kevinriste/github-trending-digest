The HN Take

Tax-exempt donations.

AnkiDroid is a free, open-source flashcard app with more than 10 million installs. It is developed by volunteers, sells nothing and is funded through donations to Open Source Collective, its fiscal host. Open Source Collective has an IRS determination letter saying it is tax-exempt under Section 501(c)(6).

Google Play's policy says its billing system "must not be used" for "tax exempt donations." It also says apps generally cannot direct users to other payment methods. So AnkiDroid linked to its Open Collective donation page and, when Google asked for proof that the recipient was tax-exempt, supplied the IRS determination letter.

Google replied:

"Specifically, your app allows users to contribute donations to an organization that is not tax-exempt."

Sure. The narrow issue here is that a 501(c)(6) organization is tax-exempt, but donations to it generally are not tax-deductible to the donor. Google's written policy says "tax exempt donations," its email gives a 501(c)(3) charity as an example, and its support team appears to have concluded that the tax-exempt organization is not tax-exempt. Possibly "tax exempt" means "tax deductible" in Google's internal policy. Possibly it means 501(c)(3). Possibly it means whatever the support email says it means after receiving the IRS letter.

The practical result is that AnkiDroid is removing the donation link from its Play Store build under protest. Otherwise Google says the app will be removed from Google Play on Sept. 11 worldwide except India and Russia.

There is a pleasingly complete platform logic here. Google Play billing must not be used for tax-exempt donations. External payment links generally must not be used either. AnkiDroid demonstrated that its fiscal host is tax-exempt, so Google declared that it isn't. Now the donations can presumably use Google Play billing, because Google has solved the tax-exemption problem.

Sixty-seven-cent intelligence.

Here is a useful AI research budget:

A researcher trained a small transformer from scratch for 1.5 hours on an Nvidia 5090 and reports that it scored 44% on ARC-AGI-1, matching TRM and HRM, plus 7% on ARC-AGI-2. The total compute cost was 67 cents.

The model is not especially exotic. It uses an eight-layer transformer, per-task embeddings, 3D rotary positional embeddings, data augmentation and test-time training. The author writes:

"I don't understand why others didn't figure this out. Its just a transformer with the most obvious representation."

There is always some danger in describing a research result as "just" an obvious idea. The benchmark has been open for six years and had a million-dollar prize, so presumably the idea was obvious mainly after someone found it. But the ablations do support the broader point. Switching from 3D to 1D positional embeddings cuts the reported score from about 44% to about 24%. Removing the per-task embeddings also cuts it to about 24%. Apparently a large part of fluid intelligence is knowing where things are and which puzzle you are doing.

There are benchmark nuances. The model trains on the evaluation puzzles but not their hidden labels, an approach the author describes as transductive reasoning. It trains across all test tasks at once, so the 67-cent cost is amortized across the full evaluation. The post devotes considerable space to explaining why this is allowed, why it is not data leakage and why comparisons with some other systems would be unfair.

Still, 67 cents. AI research is increasingly discussed as an industrial undertaking requiring giant data centers, vast power supplies and complicated financing arrangements. Here the claimed bottleneck was sample efficiency, the experiment ran on one consumer GPU, and the compute bill was less than the price of many app-store donations. The arguments about whether it counts probably consumed more resources than the model.

Analysis

Independent researcher porridgeraisin has updated their open-source transformer model to achieve a 44% score on the ARC-AGI-1 benchmark while incurring only 67 cents in compute costs. By training from scratch with a focus on sample efficiency—utilizing architectural improvements like SwiGLU, RMSNorm, and NorMuon without relying on extensive synthetic data—the project demonstrates that traditional deep learning methods can remain highly competitive on complex metalearning tasks. This work is significant because it challenges the necessity of large-scale, resource-intensive synthetic data training typically used by frontier labs to "benchmax" ARC scores. Furthermore, the project emphasizes the importance of lowering experimental costs, enabling a wider range of researchers to explore the fundamental limits of transformer-based reasoning and data efficiency.

Analysis

Azriel "Al" Blackman, a legendary aviation maintenance technician who held the Guinness World Record for the longest career as an airline mechanic, has passed away at the age of 100. Beginning his career in 1942 at American Export Airlines, Blackman spent eight decades with the company, transitioning from maintaining early Sikorsky flying boats to modern Boeing 777s.
